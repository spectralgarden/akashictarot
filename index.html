The Major Arcana

◉ Card 1

The Oracle Of Delphi

Reflect: Which part comes from evidence, and which part is a hunch?

A thoughtful way to study tarot and oracle cards

This searchable page is an independent study reference for browsing the Akashic Tarot by card name and deck section. Use a search or suit filter to find a card, read its interpretation alongside the card image and the publisher's guidebook, and note which details connect with your own question. This material is for reflection and learning; it is not a substitute for professional advice or a guarantee about future events.

How to approach a reading

  1. Choose an open-ended question about a situation, choice, or perspective you want to examine. Avoid questions that ask a card to make a consequential decision for you.
  2. Before reading an interpretation, notice the card's imagery, colors, figures, and your first response. Write down observations separately from assumptions.
  3. Use a one-card draw for a focused reflection or a three-card spread to compare a situation, a challenge, and a possible next step. Treat spread positions as prompts rather than fixed rules.
  4. Compare your notes with the reference and guidebook. Keep what is useful, question what does not fit, and revisit the reading later to see how your perspective changes.

Deck sections and reading context

The reference groups cards under the deck's Major Arcana, Scrolls, Roses, Forces, and Keys. Suit and section names help readers navigate the deck's symbolic structure, but no single keyword captures every meaning. A card can read differently depending on the question, neighboring cards, imagery, and the reader's own context.

Tarot and oracle decks are related forms of cartomancy, but their structures vary. A tarot deck often follows a recognizable Major and Minor Arcana framework; oracle decks may use their own number of cards, suits, and interpretive system. The Akashic Tarot has a distinctive structure, so compare meanings within this deck rather than assuming a standard Rider-Waite-Smith interpretation applies to every card.

Glossary for new readers

Spread
A planned arrangement of cards where each position represents a prompt or perspective.
Upright and reversed
Orientation conventions used by some readers. Reversals are optional; readers may instead consider blocked, internal, or contrasting expressions without turning a card.
Intuition
A reader's immediate associations and observations. Recording them before consulting a reference can make study more personal and less dependent on keywords.
Reflection
Using symbols to generate questions and consider choices, rather than treating a card as proof or prediction.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find a card meaning?

Search by the card's name or use the deck-section filters at the top of the page. Search results are a navigation aid; read the full card entry and consider the question and imagery in context.

Should I use reversed card meanings?

That is a reading preference, not a requirement. Some readers interpret reversals; others keep cards upright and explore tension or nuance through the question, spread position, and surrounding cards.

Can a tarot card predict exactly what will happen?

This reference does not claim that cards predict events with certainty. Use readings as prompts for reflection, and make important decisions using practical information and appropriate professional support.

Is this an official Akashic Tarot website?

No. This is an independent study reference and is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or a replacement for the deck's creators or publisher. Consult the official guidebook for the authors' intended interpretations.

How can beginners get more from a reading?

Start with one card and an open-ended question, record your own observations first, and compare them with a reference afterward. A short journal entry is often more useful than drawing many cards for the same question.
